Step 1 · Screening

Title and abstract of 25 records per call; include, exclude or unsure.

Screening task

You are screening records for a systematic review of hole-finding methods in metascience. Decide for each record below, from its title and abstract alone, whether it belongs in the review. Do not run commands, open files or search the web; everything you need is in this message.

What the review is about

Hole-finding methods try to anticipate or steer scientific progress by locating gaps in what has already been studied. They share a template. A body of research is represented as a structure with positions that can be empty: a graph (concepts linked by co-occurrence, variables linked by reported causal claims, papers linked by citation, researchers linked by co-authorship) or a matrix (populations, regions, interventions or outcomes crossed with research questions). Progress is then sought in the holes: the missing edges or the empty cells.

Examples of the family: evidence and gap maps; audits showing which populations, regions, sexes or taxa a literature has not sampled; comparisons of research effort with disease burden or societal need; literature-based discovery in the Swanson tradition; link prediction on concept or keyword networks built from papers; embedding models that predict future discoveries from the literature; systems that generate research ideas or hypotheses from the literature or a knowledge graph; structural-hole and topological analyses of knowledge networks; recommendation of new scientific collaborations; patent maps that look for vacant technology areas; measures of novel or atypical combinations in papers and patents.

Inclusion criteria

Include a record only when all three hold.

  1. Object. It is about a body of research: a literature, a field, a set of studies, patents, or researchers and their collaborations.
  2. Structure. That body of research is represented as a graph, network, matrix, map or embedding space in which positions can be unoccupied, or is compared cell by cell against an explicit benchmark such as disease burden, population share or species richness.
  3. Holes. The record locates, ranks, predicts or generates unoccupied or under-filled positions (missing links, empty or thin cells, untried combinations, absent collaborations), or evaluates or reviews methods that do.

Decisions

Give each record exactly one decision.

include, with a role:

  • method: proposes, develops, benchmarks or reviews a hole-finding method or tool.
  • application: applies one, and the holes it finds are a main result.

exclude, with the first code that applies:

  • pubtype: not a research publication with results or a method: a protocol or registration for a future map or review, an editorial, news item, peer-review report, author response, erratum, dataset or table of contents.
  • object: not about a body of research. This covers link prediction, knowledge graph completion or recommendation in general or for other domains (social media, products, proteins, drugs) with no claim about where research is or is not being done; and papers about language models that are not about generating or assessing research ideas.
  • structure: about research, but with no structured representation. The usual case is a narrative, scoping or systematic review of a topic that closes by naming "research gaps" or "future directions".
  • holes: represents a body of research structurally but only describes what is there (bibliometric overviews, trend and hotspot analyses, science maps, network statistics) without pointing at what is missing or predicting what will be added.
  • theory: a conceptual, philosophical or critical piece about research agendas, gaps or discovery with no method or application of its own. Out of scope for now.

unsure, code info: the title and abstract do not let you decide. Use it for records with no abstract whose title is ambiguous, not as a way to avoid a judgement that the text supports.

Borderline rules

  • An evidence gap map, evidence map or systematic map of a topic is an application when it reports where evidence is missing. Its protocol is pubtype.
  • A scoping or systematic review is included only if it builds an explicit map, matrix or coded database and reports gaps from it. Mentioning gaps is not enough.
  • A bibliometric study is included only if it uses the network to flag missing links, structural holes, under-researched areas or future links.
  • Drug repurposing, gene-disease prediction and similar tasks are included only when the prediction is drawn from the published literature (literature-based discovery, text-mined knowledge graphs), not from experimental or molecular data.
  • "AI scientist" and research-agent systems are included when they generate research ideas or hypotheses; excluded as object when they only automate experiments, coding or writing.
  • Studies of novelty or atypical combinations are included when they measure which combinations in papers or patents are new or rare.
  • When a record sits between method and application, choose method if a reader could reuse the procedure on another literature.
  • No abstract: decide from the title and venue if they are clear, otherwise unsure.

Output

Return one entry per record, in the order given, using each record's id unchanged. role is method or application for includes and none otherwise. code is in for includes, the exclusion code for excludes, info for unsure. note is one short sentence giving the fact in the title or abstract that decided it.

Step 2 · Annotation

Batches of kept records, rated against one another on two 1-to-11 scales.

Rating task

Below is a batch of papers on hole-finding methods in metascience: methods that represent a body of research as a graph or a matrix and look for progress in its holes, the missing links or empty cells. Rate each paper from its title and abstract.

Read the whole batch first. Then rate the papers against one another, so that differences between papers in this batch show up as differences in their ratings.

  • interp: how interpretable the paper's method is, from 1 (entirely opaque) to 11 (entirely transparent).
  • norm: how normative the paper is, from 1 (not normative at all) to 11 (clearly prescriptive).

Also give, for each paper:

  • interp_why, norm_why: a few words on what in the abstract decided each rating.
  • family: gap-map, audit (who or what a literature covers), effort-need (research effort against need), claim-graph, lbd (literature-based discovery), concept-link (link prediction on concept networks), embedding, ideation (generating research ideas), structural-hole, collaboration, patent, novelty (novel combinations), or other.
  • locality: does the method look for holes near existing work (local), far from it (distant), both (mixed), or does the question not apply (na)?
  • validation: retrospective, expert, experimental, none, or na.
  • framing: forecast, recommendation or both.
  • field, method: a few words each.
  • corpus: number of documents analysed if stated, otherwise 0.
  • summary: one plain sentence.
  • confidence: high, medium or low.

Return one entry per paper, in the order given, with its id unchanged.
