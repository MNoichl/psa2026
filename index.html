Model transfer

  • Model transfer: applying a model built from one domain in another one.
  • A key component of scientific creativity, esp. around economics.
    • Game theory to evolutionary biology, Lotka–Volterra to Goodwin’s growth cycle, econophysics (e.g. Ising models), Schelling-style ABMs.
  • Finding gaps in science to predict and prescribe:
    • Gap maps, literature-based discovery, knowledge graphs, and many more.

Part II
Gap-finding methods

Networks + LLMs for classification

  • Thoma et al.: mapping behavioral reinforcement learning research.
  • Motivation: literature growth risks duplicated efforts and disconnected research communities.
  • Representation: integrate authors, cited references, and LLM-based text similarity into one measure.
  • Application: identify communities with overlapping interests but limited exchange.
    • Similar topics + references + low author similarity → opportunities for new collaborations.
    • High semantic similarity + low reference similarity → opportunities to integrate disconnected literatures. Fig. 4
  • Model transfer: could this have predicted collaboration between psychology and behavioral economics before it began?

Thoma et al. (2025), forthcoming.

Claim graphs

  • Garg and Fetzer: Causal Claims in Economics.
  • Representation: each paper becomes a claim graph.
    • Nodes: standardized economic concepts.
    • Directed edges: claimed relationships.
    • Annotations: the evidentiary basis of each relationship.
  • Application: reconstruct the credibility revolution. Relate causal evidence, novelty, and argumentative structure to publication and citations.
  • Model transfer: could claim graphs help identify instances of model transfer? What features would a knowledge graph need to capture it?

Garg and Fetzer (2025), February 2026 revision.

Knowledge graphs + link prediction

  • Krenn et al.: forecasting the future of AI research.
  • Representation: an evolving semantic network of AI.
    • Nodes: 64,000 concepts extracted from 143,000 AI and ML papers on arXiv.
    • Edges: concepts that co-occur in a title or abstract, dated by year.
  • Application: try to predict which unconnected concept pairs will be studied together within a few years. Fig. 2
  • Main predictive signal? How connected concepts already are.
  • Model transfer: could a model and a new target domain be an unconnected pair whose future connection is predicted?

Krenn et al. (2023)

Part III
Predicting model transfer?

Some worries & Conclusion

  • Can any of these techniques actually be expected to capture model transfer?
    • Long-range transfer is hard to capture?
    • MT is based on similar conceptual structure, but how is that representable by these techniques?
    • Successful MT might be rare?
  • New forms of representation might be needed – embeddings of mathematical structure?
